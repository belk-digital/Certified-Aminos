# 10\. Recommended Schema Markup

### WebPage

Recommended. Identifies the page as a distinct, indexable entity with a clear name ("Terms and Conditions") and datePublished/dateModified values tied to the effective date shown on the page. This helps search engines and AI crawlers understand the page's purpose without misreading it as a product or blog page.

### BreadcrumbList

Recommended. The page already displays a Home \> Terms & Conditions path visually; wrapping it in BreadcrumbList schema lets Google render that trail in search results and helps AI assistants understand where the page sits in the site hierarchy.

### FAQPage

Recommended. The FAQ section above is written specifically to qualify for FAQ rich results and to be cited cleanly by generative search tools. Each question/answer pair should be marked up as a mainEntity Question/Answer node.

### Organization

Recommended, placed at the site level rather than repeated on this page. Since the Terms reference Helix Bio as a legal entity, having a single Organization schema (name, url, logo, contactPoint) elsewhere on the site strengthens entity recognition for every page that mentions the brand, including this one.

*Not recommended: Product, Review, or Offer schema — none of that data appears on a Terms page, and adding it would misrepresent the page's content to search engines.*

# 11\. GEO Optimization Notes

The copy above is written to perform well when surfaced by ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, and Microsoft Copilot:

* Each numbered section answers one clear question ("Can I cancel my order?", "Is this product approved for human use?") in self-contained paragraphs, so an AI system can quote or paraphrase a single section without needing surrounding context.

* Entities are named plainly and consistently — Helix Bio, USD, FDA — rather than referred to vaguely ("the company," "our currency"), which improves how confidently AI systems attribute the answer.

* The FAQ section mirrors natural spoken queries ("Does Helix Bio ship internationally?") rather than keyword-stuffed phrasing, matching how people actually prompt AI assistants.

* Sentences stay short and declarative in the legal sections so meaning survives summarization — a common cause of AI mis-citation is long, clause-heavy legal sentences that get paraphrased inaccurately.

# 12\. Readability & Tone Notes

The draft avoids common AI tells on purpose: no "in today's fast-paced world" openers, no rhetorical questions used as section intros, no repeated transition phrases like "furthermore" or "it is important to note." Each section leads with the operative rule, not a restatement of the heading.

Paragraphs are kept short (2–3 sentences), bullets are used only where the screenshot already used them, and the tone stays direct and slightly reassuring — a Terms page is where trust is won or lost, so hedging language and legal jargon are kept to a minimum without sacrificing enforceability.
