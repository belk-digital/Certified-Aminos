### **H1: Frequently Asked Questions**

Answers to the questions researchers ask most about peptide purity, testing, compliance, storage, and the individual compounds in the Helix Bio catalog.

## **General Peptide Education**

### **What is a research peptide?**

A research peptide is a short chain of amino acids manufactured for laboratory study rather than human or veterinary use. Helix Bio's catalog is synthesized and tested to a documented purity standard and sold exclusively for scientific research.

### **What are research peptides used for in laboratory research?**

Research peptides serve as standardized reagents in cellular, biochemical, and pharmacological studies — giving researchers a consistent, purity-verified compound to test against a defined protocol in an academic or institutional lab setting.

### **How are research peptides classified for human use?**

They are not classified for human use at all. Every compound in the Helix Bio catalog is labeled Research Use Only (RUO), meaning it is not evaluated, approved, or intended for human or veterinary consumption.

## **Legality & Compliance**

### **Are research peptides legal to purchase in the USA?**

Yes, research peptides sold and labeled strictly for laboratory research are legal to purchase in the USA. Legality depends on the RUO labeling and intended use being accurately maintained by both supplier and buyer.

### **What makes a peptide “research use only”?**

A peptide is designated research use only when it is manufactured, labeled, and sold exclusively for scientific study — not for diagnostic, therapeutic, or human-consumption purposes, and without FDA evaluation for those other uses.

### **How are research peptides regulated?**

Research peptides are not regulated as drugs or supplements. They fall under general chemical and research-reagent handling rather than pharmaceutical approval pathways, which is why accurate RUO labeling and documentation matter so much.

## **Quality & Analytical Testing**

### **What is a certificate of analysis (COA)?**

A COA is a batch-specific document reporting a peptide's measured purity (via HPLC), confirmed molecular weight (via mass spectrometry), and testing date. It lets a researcher verify a vial's specifications independently rather than take a label on faith.

### **How do I verify peptide purity through analytical testing?**

Purity is verified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), which separates the target compound from related impurities and reports a purity percentage. Mass spectrometry is used alongside it to confirm the compound's identity and molecular weight.

### **What purity level should a research peptide meet?**

Reputable suppliers report purity of 98% or higher, with many premium research compounds tested at 99% or above. The figure should come from an independent batch test, not an unverified marketing claim.

### **How can I verify a peptide's purity independently?**

Request the batch-specific certificate of analysis and match its lot number to the vial received. Researchers with in-house or third-party lab access can also run their own HPLC or mass spectrometry confirmation.

## **Supplier & Ordering Questions**

### **How do I choose a reliable peptide supplier?**

Look for a supplier that tests every batch independently, publishes the COA before purchase, states a clear purity threshold, and keeps research-use labeling visible rather than downplaying it near checkout.

### **What questions should I ask a peptide supplier?**

Ask whether every batch is independently tested, whether a COA is available before you order, what purity threshold is guaranteed, and how orders are shipped and stored in transit.

### **What documentation should be included with a peptide order?**

At minimum, an order should include an invoice and access to the batch-specific certificate of analysis. Institutional buyers can request additional documentation, such as consolidated invoicing, through a wholesale account.

### **Do you offer any product bundles or discounts?**

Current promotions, bundles, and discount programs are listed on the shop page rather than duplicated in the FAQ, since availability changes with inventory. Check the shop or subscribe to the mailing list for updates.

## **Storage & Handling**

### **How should I store lyophilized peptides?**

Unreconstituted, lyophilized peptides are generally stored frozen or refrigerated, protected from light and moisture, until needed for a study. Storage guidance specific to a compound is included on its product page.

### **Why is temperature control important for peptide stability?**

Heat exposure and repeated freeze-thaw cycles can degrade a peptide's structure and reduce its usable purity over time. Consistent, appropriate temperature control preserves the integrity confirmed on the certificate of analysis.

### **What is cold-chain handling?**

Cold-chain handling means a compound is kept within a controlled temperature range from shipment through delivery, using insulated packaging and, where needed, cold packs, so stability isn't compromised in transit.

### **General Peptide Education**

**1\. What is a research peptide?**

A research peptide is a short chain of amino acids manufactured for laboratory study rather than human or veterinary use. Helix Bio's catalog is synthesized and tested to a documented purity standard and sold exclusively for scientific research.

**2\. What are research peptides used for in laboratory research?**

Research peptides serve as standardized reagents in cellular, biochemical, and pharmacological studies — giving researchers a consistent, purity-verified compound to test against a defined protocol in an academic or institutional lab setting.

**3\. How are research peptides classified for human use?**

They are not classified for human use at all. Every compound in the Helix Bio catalog is labeled Research Use Only (RUO), meaning it is not evaluated, approved, or intended for human or veterinary consumption.

### **Legality & Compliance**

**4\. Are research peptides legal to purchase in the USA?**

Yes, research peptides sold and labeled strictly for laboratory research are legal to purchase in the USA. Legality depends on the RUO labeling and intended use being accurately maintained by both supplier and buyer.

**5\. What makes a peptide “research use only”?**

A peptide is designated research use only when it is manufactured, labeled, and sold exclusively for scientific study — not for diagnostic, therapeutic, or human-consumption purposes, and without FDA evaluation for those other uses.

**6\. How are research peptides regulated?**

Research peptides are not regulated as drugs or supplements. They fall under general chemical and research-reagent handling rather than pharmaceutical approval pathways, which is why accurate RUO labeling and documentation matter so much.

### **Quality & Analytical Testing**

**7\. What is a certificate of analysis (COA)?**

A COA is a batch-specific document reporting a peptide's measured purity (via HPLC), confirmed molecular weight (via mass spectrometry), and testing date. It lets a researcher verify a vial's specifications independently rather than take a label on faith.

**8\. How do I verify peptide purity through analytical testing?**

Purity is verified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), which separates the target compound from related impurities and reports a purity percentage. Mass spectrometry is used alongside it to confirm the compound's identity and molecular weight.

**9\. What purity level should a research peptide meet?**

Reputable suppliers report purity of 98% or higher, with many premium research compounds tested at 99% or above. The figure should come from an independent batch test, not an unverified marketing claim.

**10\. How can I verify a peptide's purity independently?**

Request the batch-specific certificate of analysis and match its lot number to the vial received. Researchers with in-house or third-party lab access can also run their own HPLC or mass spectrometry confirmation.

### **Supplier & Ordering Questions**

**11\. How do I choose a reliable peptide supplier?**

Look for a supplier that tests every batch independently, publishes the COA before purchase, states a clear purity threshold, and keeps research-use labeling visible rather than downplaying it near checkout.

**12\. What questions should I ask a peptide supplier?**

Ask whether every batch is independently tested, whether a COA is available before you order, what purity threshold is guaranteed, and how orders are shipped and stored in transit.

**13\. What documentation should be included with a peptide order?**

At minimum, an order should include an invoice and access to the batch-specific certificate of analysis. Institutional buyers can request additional documentation, such as consolidated invoicing, through a wholesale account.

**14\. Do you offer any product bundles or discounts?**

Current promotions, bundles, and discount programs are listed on the shop page rather than duplicated in the FAQ, since availability changes with inventory. Check the shop or subscribe to the mailing list for updates.

### **Storage & Handling**

**15\. How should I store lyophilized peptides?**

Unreconstituted, lyophilized peptides are generally stored frozen or refrigerated, protected from light and moisture, until needed for a study. Storage guidance specific to a compound is included on its product page.

**16\. Why is temperature control important for peptide stability?**

Heat exposure and repeated freeze-thaw cycles can degrade a peptide's structure and reduce its usable purity over time. Consistent, appropriate temperature control preserves the integrity confirmed on the certificate of analysis.

**17\. What is cold-chain handling?**

Cold-chain handling means a compound is kept within a controlled temperature range from shipment through delivery, using insulated packaging and, where needed, cold packs, so stability isn't compromised in transit.
